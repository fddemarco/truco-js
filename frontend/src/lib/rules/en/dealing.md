# Deck and dealing

## 5. Mazo, shuffling, cutting and dealing

- **5.1 Official mazo.** 40-card Spanish deck, official or authorized. Players' own cards are not allowed.
- **5.2 First dealer.** Each player draws a random card; the one who draws the **highest truco card** deals (Anexo A). If several tie for the highest, only those tied draw again. The cards go back into the mazo before shuffling.
- **5.3 Shuffling.** Shuffle enough; marking, bending, holding back or arranging cards is forbidden.
- **5.4 Mandatory cut.** Made by the **rival immediately to the left** of the dealer, before every deal. It can be done on the table (the reference method, which the cutter may demand) or in the dealer's hand. It must split the mazo into 2 parts of **more than 3 cards each**. Cutting into several piles or just tapping the mazo is not valid. Deliberately refusing to do what the referee says → disciplinary warning.
- **5.5 Dealing.** 3 cards to each player, **one at a time**, starting with the player to the dealer's **right** and continuing to the right. Each dealer chooses to deal from the **top or the bottom** of the mazo; that method is fixed from their first deal of the partido (even if repeated) and for all their later deals. Using the other method = dealing error (the deal is repeated, or 6.4 applies if it is their 2nd error). At the end, the mazo stays to the dealer's right (which shows who is mano).
- **5.6 No touching during the deal.** Nobody touches their cards until the dealer gives the 3rd card to the last player; from then on everyone may look at them (also in pica-pica, even if their duelo has not started). If a card meant for a rival is exposed, nobody looks at their cards until it is settled under 6.1.
- **5.7 Rotation.** At the end of each mano, the player to the **right** of the previous dealer deals (unless a special rule says to repeat).
- **5.8 Defective mazo.** Before the mano: it is replaced or fixed. During the mano, if it may have affected play: the referee may annul the mano; earlier manos stand unless there was deliberate manipulation.

## 6. Dealing errors and irregularities

- **6.1 Dealer errors** (among others): exposing a card meant for a **rival**, dealing too many or too few cards, skipping a player, leaving cards whose owner cannot be determined, switching between top and bottom, altering the deal. Exposing **one's own or a teammate's** card **is not an error** and the deal continues.
  - If a card meant for a rival is exposed: the deal stops and the error is recorded. If it is the **1st error of the cycle**, the captain of the affected team decides whether to **continue** (the exposed card still belongs to its recipient) or **repeat** (the same dealer shuffles, offers the cut and deals). Either way the error **is recorded**. If anyone looks at their cards before that decision, that deal cannot continue (6.7/6.8).
  - Each further rival card exposed adds another error, even in the same deal. On reaching the **2nd error of the cycle**, 6.4 applies immediately, even if the first error was of a different kind.
  - Different irregularities in the same deal may count as separate errors.
- **6.2 Correctable disorder.** If the owner of each card can be known for certain and both captains agree, the cards are rearranged and play continues. It counts as an error, unless validated as a minor irregularity (6.9).
- **6.3 Uncorrectable disorder.** The deal is void, the error is counted and the cards are dealt again.
- **6.4 Dealer error cycle.**
  - **1st error** → recorded; if the deal must be repeated, **the same** player deals again.
  - **2nd error** by the same dealer in the cycle → **+2 points to the rival**, the **mano is annulled** and the mazo passes to the **next dealer**. It applies even if both errors were in the same deal or the rival agreed to continue.
  - After it applies, that dealer's counter goes back to 0 and a new cycle starts. Counters are **individual per dealer** and reset every partido.
- **6.5 Wrong number of cards.** It must be reported as soon as it is noticed and before any action to gain an advantage; it is not fixed by discarding or taking cards. If discovered during the mano and in good faith: the mano is annulled, it counts as a dealer error and the cards are dealt again, with no sanction to the player. A closed mano is not reopened (unless later evidence of concealment appears).
- **6.6 Deliberate concealment** of a wrong number of cards to gain an advantage → their team **loses the partido**. A 2nd time in the qualifying tournament → **disqualification** (regardless of which player did it).
- **6.7 Improper handling during the deal** (touching or looking too early, looking while the 6.1 decision is pending, looking at others' cards). If accidental and correctable, things are restored without sanction (there may be a verbal warning). If it causes disorder or exposes cards, the deal may be annulled and the responsibility lies with the handler, not the dealer.
- **6.8 Repetition.** Doing it again after a verbal warning → possible disciplinary warning. There is **no** individual technical counter or +2 for these behaviours. If deliberate or fraudulent → direct sanction. The 6.4 regime is independent.
- **6.9 Validating minor accidental irregularities.** They can be validated by agreement of both teams (in a mano redonda the captains decide; in a duelo de pica-pica, the two contenders). If play continues knowingly, the irregularity is validated and cannot be claimed later.
  - **Cannot be validated:** fraud, carteo, collusion, deliberate outside help, marked cards, intentional manipulation of the mazo, assaults, threats, safety, integrity.
  - **Always recorded and counted** (even with agreement): exposing cards meant for the rival and switching between top and bottom.
  - Validating something as accidental does not prevent a later review if evidence shows it was deliberate.
